# If something goes wrong

Most problems are disagreements about condition, where the card should simply go back. Escade handles that case first and separately, because it is by far the most common.

## Sending it back: returns

**In a sale,** if the card arrives and it is not what was described, the buyer can start a return instead of confirming receipt, any time in the 3-day inspection window. The seller does not have to agree; the return goes ahead automatically.

1. **The buyer starts the return.** The seller is not paid while the return is open.
2. **The buyer posts the card back** within 7 days and enters the tracking number.
3. **The seller confirms the card has come back.** The buyer is refunded the price and the delivery fee.

If the seller goes quiet once the card has been delivered back, the buyer is refunded automatically. If the buyer starts a return and never posts the card, the sale completes and the seller is paid.

A seller who thinks the card that came back is not the one they sent, or not in the condition it left in, can raise a dispute.

On a return each side has paid for one leg of postage: the seller sent the card, the buyer sends it back. That is why the delivery fee is refunded to the buyer.

**In a trade,** each card is its own sale, so the same rules apply to each side separately. If the card you received is not as described, you return it and are refunded what you paid for it. The other side of the trade carries on as normal.

No one else needs to be involved. This is the route we expect most problems to take, and it is the one where everybody walks away whole.

## When you cannot agree: disputes

A dispute pauses the payment for that card, and Escade is notified. A buyer can raise a dispute once the card has been posted and before the inspection window closes. A seller can raise one about a returned card.

**Escade reviews the evidence and decides.** We look at what both sides have submitted, the listing, the tracking records and each trader's history. We then decide one of three outcomes:

- the buyer is refunded in full, with the card returned where that makes sense;
- the buyer is refunded in part, and keeps the card;
- the seller is paid in full.

The decision is recorded with the reasons and a timestamp. Our payment processing supplier then carries it out. We aim to decide within 7 days of the dispute being raised.

## What we can rule on

Being straight about this matters more than sounding capable.

**Strong evidence:** who listed, who bought or accepted, who posted and when, who confirmed, what tracking said, what the listing claimed. All of this is timestamped.

**Weak evidence:** what condition the card was in when it went into the envelope. We were not there. A tracking number proves a parcel was delivered, not what was in it.

So Escade rules confidently on performance — did you post it, did you post it on time, did you confirm, was it damaged in transit. It rules much less confidently on condition, and the dispute process puts the burden of proof on whoever raised the dispute: if the evidence does not settle the question, the decision goes against the party who brought it. That rule exists so that a complaint nobody can verify is not a free option.

## Chargebacks

Card payments can be disputed with your bank. If you have a genuine problem, please raise it on Escade first: it is quicker, and we hold the payment so we can actually refund you.

If someone receives a card and then reverses their payment through their bank in bad faith, we give the bank the evidence we hold, including tracking and delivery records. Accounts that do this are closed, and because everyone who is paid on Escade is identity-checked, we can pass details to the police if the person who lost out reports it.
