# Payments and fees

## How payments are held

When you buy a card, or accept a trade, you pay up front. That payment is held by our payment and transaction processing supplier, not passed to the seller. The seller is paid only once the card has arrived and the 3-day inspection window has closed with no problem raised.

In a trade, both of you pay the agreed price of the cards you will receive. When the trade completes, each of you is paid for the cards you sent, so the money cancels out. The only difference you will see is the time between paying and being paid.

Holding the full payment does the job that collateral used to.

**It makes backing out cost something.** A trader who receives a card and never sends theirs has paid full price for the card they kept, and that money goes to the trader they let down. A seller who never posts is not paid, and the buyer is refunded in full.

**It makes a promise worth something.** Research on used-goods auctions found that letting a seller make a specific promise raised the rate at which buyers trusted them and, more importantly, the rate at which sellers followed through. A condition grade on Escade is backed by the seller only being paid once the buyer has checked the card.

**It makes a false dispute cost something.** If you raise a dispute and the evidence does not support it, the decision goes against you.

**It makes fake reputation expensive.** On eBay, a feedback point once cost about 29 cents to manufacture, because a transaction could be notional. On Escade, faking one means two identity-checked accounts, listing fees paid, real money held for the length of the trade, and a card actually posted.

Whilst we are starting out, listings are capped at £50, and in a trade the cap applies to each side's cards. New accounts may have lower limits, which rise as they build a trading history.

## Fees

**Listing fee.** Paid when the listing is created, by the seller or by whoever creates the trade. It is 5% of the listing's value, or for a trade, of the value of the cards you are asking for. It is not refunded if the listing is cancelled.

**Delivery fee.** In a sale, the buyer pays the delivery fee at checkout. It is held with the price and paid to the seller with it, since the seller buys postage outside Escade for now. If the card is returned, the delivery fee goes back to the buyer, because the buyer has then paid for the return leg. In a trade, each side buys their own postage, and nothing is held for it.

**Return postage.** Bought by whoever sends the card back. See [If something goes wrong](disputes.md).

The listing fee is the only fee Escade takes. There is no fee for raising a dispute, and no fee taken from a decision.

## When you get paid

| Situation | Outcome |
| --- | --- |
| Card arrives, no problem raised in 3 days | Seller is paid in full |
| Seller never posts | Buyer is refunded in full |
| Card returned as not as described | Buyer is refunded price and delivery |
| Trade: first trader never posts | Trade cancelled, both refunded |
| Trade: second trader never posts | First trader is refunded and paid for the card they sent |
| Dispute decided | Buyer refunded in full or in part, or seller paid |

Payouts go to the bank account you verified with our payment processing supplier. Refunds go back to the card you paid with, and usually appear within 5 to 10 working days.
