# Known limitations

Written in our own words, because you will find most of these out anyway and it is better that you find them here.

**Escade cannot verify an item's condition or what was received.** The dispute process is strong on whether you posted a card and when, and weak on what state it was in. If your complaint rests entirely on condition, in a sale the buyer can always send the card back for a refund instead, and the seller cannot refuse the return. If two parties disagree on what was posted and received, understand that we are deciding based on descriptions, evidence and history, not on the card.

**The dispute process depends on a single decision-maker.** That is Escade. The academic literature is clear that a single internal arbiter is the weakest link in any design like this, and the honest answer is that it is a trade-off: the alternative is a panel, which is slow and expensive. What we have done instead is publish the rules we decide by, put the burden of proof on whoever raises a dispute, and record every decision with its reasons.

**Card payments can be reversed.** A buyer can dispute a card payment with their bank, sometimes weeks after a sale. We fight bad-faith chargebacks with tracking and delivery evidence, and close the accounts behind them, but we cannot stop someone trying. This is a limit every card marketplace shares.

**A bad reputation is harder to abandon, but not impossible.** Everyone who is paid on Escade verifies their identity, so walking away from one account to start a fresh one is much harder than with an email address alone. It is not impossible, and someone could build up a good trading history before letting someone down once. Limits that rise slowly with history keep the size of that one let-down small.

**Your money is tied up during a trade.** In a trade you pay the full agreed price of the cards you will receive, and you are paid for yours only once both cards have arrived and been checked. For a few weeks, that money is not available to you.

**The parameters are estimates.** The listing fee (5%), the time limits (3 to 21 days) and the account limits are our current best guesses, not figures derived from how the market behaves. We do not have enough transaction history yet to set them from evidence, and we intend to revise them once we do.

**Escade is not an insurer.** Your protection comes from the payment being held until the card has arrived and been checked. Beyond that there is no guarantee and no protection fund. We may choose to compensate someone in a case that was clearly fraud, as a one-off decision, and you should not plan around it.

---

If any of this is a dealbreaker, that is a reasonable conclusion to reach, and we would rather you reached it now.
